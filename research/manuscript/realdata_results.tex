\subsection{Age-related spectral associations}
The primary age analysis includes 78 participants, of whom 41 are women, with ages ranging from 25 to 101 years and a median age of 57 years. There are 153 recording periods and 3,059 retained stage-2 Fpz--Cz segments. One selected segment is excluded by the quality rule. Table~\ref{tab:eeg} gives the corresponding counts and tests for the other stage--channel combinations.

Age is associated with both the quantile spectral surface and ordinary log power in the primary analysis, with global $p<0.001$ for each. Figure~\ref{fig:age} shows negative age coefficients around the spindle-frequency region at the displayed quantiles. At 13 Hz and $\tau=0.5$, the estimated coefficient is $-0.072$ per decade, with a simultaneous interval of $[-0.120,-0.025]$ over the full primary surface. The corresponding ordinary log-power coefficient is $-0.171$, with a simultaneous interval of $[-0.257,-0.085]$ over its frequency curve. These coefficients describe different responses. The rank-based estimate concerns temporal ordering relative to the within-segment amplitude distribution, while the ordinary estimate also retains physical amplitude variation.

The primary Fpz--Cz analysis does not show clear evidence that the age coefficient differs across quantile levels. Its direct heterogeneity test has an unadjusted $p=0.181$ and a Holm-adjusted $p=0.315$. The secondary Pz--Oz analysis shows a different pattern. For stage 2, the global heterogeneity test has $p=0.001$, or $0.003$ after Holm adjustment across the four stage--channel heterogeneity tests in the age study. At 13 Hz, the difference between the $\tau=0.1$ and median age coefficients is $0.017$, with a simultaneous interval of $[0.004,0.029]$ jointly over all eight nonmedian contrasts and 60 frequencies within the stage-2 Pz--Oz analysis. The estimated age association is therefore less negative at the lower quantile than at the median in this region. REM Pz--Oz also shows evidence of quantile heterogeneity after this adjustment. The Pz--Oz analyses provide evidence of age associations that differ across quantile levels.

Accounting for repeated records has a substantial effect on uncertainty. In the primary quantile analysis, the median ratio of the subject-level standard error to the standard error obtained by treating segments as independent is 4.04 across the grid. The regression point estimates are the same in this comparison. The difference is the amount of independent information assigned to the repeated observations.

\subsection{Paired temazepam and placebo nights}
All 22 participants in the Sleep Telemetry study contribute both conditions to the primary analysis, giving 44 nights and 874 retained stage-2 Fpz--Cz segments. There are 15 women, and ages range from 18 to 79 years. Six selected segments are excluded by the channel-specific quality rule. The paired design compares spectral summaries within the same person while adjusting for recording period and differences in the timing of the selected segments.

Both the quantile surface and ordinary log-power curve differ between temazepam and placebo nights, with global $p<0.001$ for each. The rank-based contrasts are negative at low frequencies and positive over much of the higher-frequency range (Figure~\ref{fig:paired}). At $\tau=0.5$, the contrast at 13 Hz is $0.314$, with a simultaneous interval of $[0.184,0.445]$ over the quantile surface. At 2 Hz it is $-0.055$, with interval $[-0.081,-0.029]$. Ordinary log power also increases around the spindle-frequency region, while its reduction extends over a broader low-frequency range. A positive rank-spectral contrast represents a higher adjusted mean log quantile spectral summary at the specified frequency.

The paired application identifies frequency-dependent changes in the temporal ordering of signal values within the same sleep stage and person. These rank-based differences accompany changes in ordinary power and describe more than an amplitude rescaling of the recordings. The primary heterogeneity test has an unadjusted $p=0.091$ and a Holm-adjusted $p=0.272$; none of the four paired analyses detects quantile heterogeneity after adjustment. The evidence therefore concerns a spectral contrast across conditions, without resolving differences in that contrast across amplitude quantiles. These contrasts are conditional on sleep stage and recording times.

\subsection{Sensitivity to spectral resolution}
The main age and paired associations remain detectable when the Gaussian bandwidth is changed to 1 or 2 Hz and when the first 30 seconds of each selected interval are analyzed. The coefficient profiles retain the main negative age association near the spindle region and the opposing low- and high-frequency medication contrasts. Detailed tests and coefficient curves are given in the appendix. Because changing window length or bandwidth also changes the finite-resolution target, agreement in the broad profiles is the relevant comparison; the coefficients need not be identical.

The age results describe adjusted between-person associations, and the medication results describe paired, stage-specific contrasts. Both use the retained recordings under the stated quality criteria.
