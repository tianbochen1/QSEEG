\section{Theoretical properties}\label{sec:theory}
\subsection{Invariance and the independent sampling unit}
The rank construction and the subject weighting give two finite-sample properties.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:invariance}
For a fixed collection of retained segments, covariates, weights, and grid points, replacing each segment $X_{ij,t}$ by $g_{ij}(X_{ij,t})$, where $g_{ij}$ is strictly increasing, leaves $Y_{ij}$, $\widehat\beta$, and the bootstrap inference unchanged. If each segment of a subject is duplicated the same number of times and its weight is divided by that number, then $\widehat\beta$, $Q_i$, and $\widehat U_i$ are unchanged. The uncorrected sandwich covariance, with $c_N=1$, is also unchanged by this duplication.
\end{proposition}

The duplication property makes explicit why repeating an existing record does not increase the independent information in the analysis. The small-sample correction in \eqref{eq:sandwich} depends on $M$ and is not included in that exact invariance statement.

\subsection{Regression asymptotics and bootstrap validity}
Allow the segment counts $m_{i,N}$, segment length $n_N$, and bandwidth $h_N$ to vary with the number of subjects $N$. A subscript $N$ denotes the resulting row of the triangular array, including its finite-resolution regression target $\beta_N$. Write $U_{i,N}(s)=\sum_jw_{ij,N}x_{ij,N}\{Y_{ij,N}(s)-x_{ij,N}\trans\beta_N(s)\}$ and $\Omega_N(s,s')=\E\{U_{i,N}(s)U_{i,N}(s')\trans\}$. The cluster $\mathcal C_{i,N}$ includes all observations, covariates, counts, and weights from subject $i$. Vector norms are Euclidean and matrix norms are operator norms.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A1)] Within each row, the clusters $\mathcal C_{i,N}$ are independent and identically distributed. The number of covariates $p$, the grid $\mathcal G$, and $\epsilon>0$ are fixed.
\item[(A2)] As $N\to\infty$, $Q_N=\E Q_{i,N}\to Q\succ0$ and $\Omega_N(s,s')\to\Omega(s,s')$ for all $s,s'\in\mathcal G$. For some $\eta>0$, $\sup_N\E\|Q_{i,N}\|^{2+\eta}<\infty$ and $\sup_N\E\{\max_{s\in\mathcal G}\|U_{i,N}(s)\|^{2+\eta}\}<\infty$.
\item[(A3)] $a\trans Q^{-1}\Omega(s,s)Q^{-1}a>0$ for every contrast and grid point in the simultaneous family.
\end{enumerate}

Segments within a subject may be dependent. With weights summing to one, these conditions allow bounded or increasing $m_{i,N}$ and $n_N$ without a separate restriction on their rates relative to $N$. The uniform moment and covariance conditions must still hold. At fixed $n$ the rank spectral response is bounded; this alone does not establish uniform moments when $n_N$ grows.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:clt}
Under (A1)--(A2), as $N\to\infty$, the regression estimator satisfies, jointly over $s\in\mathcal G$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear}
 \sqrt N\{\widehat\beta_N(s)-\beta_N(s)\}
 =Q^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{i=1}^N U_{i,N}(s)+\op(1).
\end{equation}
Its $pD$-dimensional stacked limit is mean-zero Gaussian with covariance blocks $V(s,s')=Q^{-1}\Omega(s,s')Q^{-1}$. Moreover, $\max_{s,s'\in\mathcal G}\|\widehat V_N(s,s')-V(s,s')\|\to0$ in probability.
\end{theorem}

The expansion in \eqref{eq:linear} makes the subject the independent unit. Additional segments can improve precision, while persistent subject heterogeneity can retain a nonzero variance at the $\sqrt N$ scale. For example, the grand mean of $N$ subjects with $m$ segments each and independent subject and segment effects has variance $\sigma_B^2/N+\sigma_E^2/(Nm)$, where $\sigma_B^2$ and $\sigma_E^2$ are their respective variances. If a contrast's variance at the $\sqrt N$ scale tends to zero, (A3) fails and a different normalization is needed. Theorem~\ref{thm:clt} also applies to subject-level paired differences.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bootstrap}
Suppose (A1)--(A3) hold and the bootstrap is constructed as in \eqref{eq:wild}. Let
$Z_N=\max_{s\in\mathcal G}|a\trans\{\widehat\beta_N(s)-\beta_N(s)\}|/\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_N(s)$.
If $\Pp^*$ denotes probability conditional on the data, then, as $N\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bootstrap-valid}
 \sup_{z\in\R}\left|\Pp^*(T_N^*\leq z)-\Pp(Z_N\leq z)\right|
 \longrightarrow 0
 \quad\text{in probability}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed $0<\alpha<1$, the simultaneous intervals in \eqref{eq:band} cover $a\trans\beta_N$ with probability tending to $1-\alpha$. The global test has limiting level $\alpha$ under $H_0$ and is consistent for alternatives satisfying $\liminf_N\max_{s\in\mathcal G}|a\trans\beta_N(s)-\theta_0(s)|>0$.
\end{theorem}

The approximation in \eqref{eq:bootstrap-valid} concerns the complete specified grid and permits a singular covariance matrix for the full surface, provided each tested contrast has positive limiting variance. The coverage statement uses the ideal conditional bootstrap quantile; Monte Carlo intervals have the same limit when $B_{\mathrm{boot}}\to\infty$ as $N\to\infty$. The proofs are given in the appendix.

\subsection{Relation to a latent spectral regression}
Let $\beta_N^0$ be the projection defined with $L^0_{ij,N}$, using the same covariates and weights. Put $e^{\mathrm{sp}}_{ij,N}(s)=Y_{ij,N}(s)-L^0_{ij,N}(s)$ and $A_{i,N}(s)=\sum_jw_{ij,N}x_{ij,N}e^{\mathrm{sp}}_{ij,N}(s)$. The target difference is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target-bias}
 \beta_N(s)-\beta_N^0(s)=Q_N^{-1}\E A_{i,N}(s).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:target-bias} distinguishes a spectral bias absorbed by the intercept from one associated with a covariate that changes its slope. Let $\widetilde\beta_N^0$ be the estimator in \eqref{eq:estimator} with $L^0_{ij,N}$ in place of $Y_{ij,N}$, and define $U^0_{i,N}(s)=\sum_jw_{ij,N}x_{ij,N}\{L^0_{ij,N}(s)-x_{ij,N}\trans\beta_N^0(s)\}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:transfer}
Suppose (A1)--(A2) hold for the augmented clusters containing the observed and latent responses, with $U^0_{i,N}$ in place of $U_{i,N}$. If, as $N\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer-conditions}
 \sqrt N\max_{s\in\mathcal G}\|\E A_{i,N}(s)\|\to0,
 \qquad
 \E\left[\max_{s\in\mathcal G}
 \|A_{i,N}(s)-\E A_{i,N}(s)\|^2\right]\to0,
\end{equation}
then $\max_{s\in\mathcal G}\sqrt N\|\widehat\beta_N(s)-\widetilde\beta_N^0(s)\|\to0$ in probability as $N\to\infty$. The observed and oracle sandwich covariances are asymptotically equivalent. If the oracle contrast variances have positive limits, the intervals in \eqref{eq:band} cover $a\trans\beta_N^0$ with probability tending to $1-\alpha$.
\end{theorem}

In \eqref{eq:transfer-conditions}, the mean spectral error must be negligible at the subject inference rate, whereas the centered error need only vanish in mean square. Individual spectral consistency does not ensure the first condition. The next result states sufficient conditions linking $N$, $m_{i,N}$, and $n_N$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:growth}
Assume (A1)--(A2) with oracle scores $U^0_{i,N}$, and $w_{ij,N}\leq C/m_{i,N}$ for a constant $C$ independent of $N$, with $m_{i,N}\geq\underline m_N\geq1$. Let $\mathcal H_{i,N}$ contain the latent spectra, covariates, counts, and weights. Write $\widehat S_{ij,N}$ for \eqref{eq:est-spectrum} at length $n_N$ and bandwidth $h_N$. For $d_{ij,N}=\widehat S_{ij,N}-S_{ij,N}$, suppose uniformly in $i,j,s$ that, for deterministic $b_N,v_N\geq0$,
$|\E(d_{ij,N}(s)\mid\mathcal H_{i,N})|\leq b_N$ and $\E\{d_{ij,N}(s)^2\mid\mathcal H_{i,N}\}\leq v_N$.
Suppose also that the centered log errors $e^{\mathrm{c}}_{ij,N}(s)=e^{\mathrm{sp}}_{ij,N}(s)-\E\{e^{\mathrm{sp}}_{ij,N}(s)\mid\mathcal H_{i,N}\}$ satisfy
\[
 \max_j\sum_{k=1}^{m_{i,N}}
 \left|\cov\{e^{\mathrm{c}}_{ij,N}(s),e^{\mathrm{c}}_{ik,N}(s)\mid\mathcal H_{i,N}\}\right|
 \leq Cv_N.
\]
Then, as $N\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth-rate}
 \max_{s\in\mathcal G}\|\widehat\beta_N(s)-\widetilde\beta_N^0(s)\|
 =\Op\left(b_N+v_N+\sqrt{\frac{v_N}{N\underline m_N}}\right).
\end{equation}
If $\sqrt N(b_N+v_N)\to0$, the conclusions of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} hold, with its positivity condition for coverage.
\end{corollary}

For equal-night weights, the weight bound in Corollary~\ref{cor:growth} holds exactly when $m_{i,N}/(K_{i,N}\min_k m_{ik,N})$, the ratio of the average to the smallest nightly segment count, is uniformly bounded. The factor $1/\underline m_N$ in \eqref{eq:growth-rate} requires the conditional covariance bound; it need not hold under arbitrary segment dependence. The mean log error is not divided by the segment count. Conditional independence of segment errors implies the covariance bound by the log-error inequalities proved in the appendix.

\begin{corollary}[Joint growth]\label{cor:ratios}
Retain the oracle, weighting, and conditional moment and covariance assumptions of Corollary~\ref{cor:growth}. Suppose the oracle contrast variances have positive limits, $b_N=O\{h_N^2+(n_Nh_N)^{-1}\}$, $v_N=O\{h_N^4+(n_Nh_N)^{-1}\}$, and $h_N=n_N^{-1/3}$. Let $N,m_N,n_N\to\infty$, with $cm_N\leq m_{i,N}\leq Cm_N$ almost surely for constants $0<c\leq C<\infty$, and
\[
 \frac{m_N}{N}\to\kappa\in[0,\infty],
 \qquad
 \frac{n_N}{N}\to\rho\in(0,\infty].
\]
Here an infinite limit means that the corresponding ratio diverges. Then $\sqrt N(\widehat\beta_N-\widetilde\beta_N^0)=\op(1)$ jointly over $\mathcal G$. Thus $\sqrt N(\widehat\beta_N-\beta_N^0)$ has the centered Gaussian limit obtained by applying Theorem~\ref{thm:clt} to the oracle responses. The sandwich covariance consistently estimates the covariance of this limit, and the bootstrap intervals in \eqref{eq:band} have limiting coverage $1-\alpha$ for $a\trans\beta_N^0$.
\end{corollary}

The spectral moment rates in Corollary~\ref{cor:ratios} are additional assumptions, not consequences of the smoothing construction alone. They require verification for the chosen conditional time-series model; \citet{Kley2016,Birr2017} give related quantile spectral theory. For this bandwidth, the broader sufficient relation is $N=o(n_N^{4/3})$, even with bounded segment counts. The ratio $m_N/N$ does not constrain the transfer: its stated limits describe admissible designs, while the inference rate also requires positive oracle contrast variances.
