\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Regression of rank-based spectral summaries provides a distribution-sensitive description of repeated EEG recordings while keeping the subject as the unit of inference. Subject-normalized weights specify each person's contribution to the regression target, and the cluster scores retain dependence across nights, segments, frequencies, and quantile levels. The studentized wild bootstrap uses this joint structure to form simultaneous confidence bands and global tests.

The simulations show why the weighting scheme and the covariance calculation must be considered separately. Treating segments as independent can give narrow bands with poor coverage, while weighting all segments equally can change the target when recording counts are informative. Retaining segment-level covariates can improve precision relative to subject averaging. The volatility experiments identify a setting in which nonmedian quantile spectra respond to changes that leave the population ordinary spectrum unchanged.

The sleep EEG analyses distinguish between-person age associations from within-person medication contrasts under a common spectral measurement procedure. Within the parietal--occipital recordings, the age association varies across quantile levels. The paired analysis localizes frequency-dependent differences between medication and placebo nights. The rank-based contrasts describe temporal ordering relative to each segment's amplitude distribution, while ordinary power also reflects the amplitude scale.

The theoretical results establish inference for a fixed analysis grid as the number of subjects increases. A latent-spectrum interpretation requires the additional bias and variance conditions stated in the paper. Extensions to cross-channel copula spectra would incorporate phase and lead--lag dependence, while a locally stationary formulation could describe changes within longer recordings. Analyses with few subjects also warrant further study of small-sample calibration beyond the settings considered here.
